Tunic

A love letter to classic instruction manuals wrapped in an isometric adventure that trusts you to figure things out.

sports_esports Developer Andrew Shouldice · calendar_today Release 2022 · business Publisher Finji · devices Platform PC, Xbox, Switch

Playing Tunic feels like being a kid again, discovering a Japanese import game without knowing the language. It’s a game built around the joy of not knowing — and the thrill of figuring things out.

The Manual Is the Game

The central mechanic is brilliant: you collect pages of an in-game instruction manual written in a fictional language. Each page reveals game mechanics you might have been able to do all along — you just didn’t know it yet. It turns game literacy into a puzzle itself.

The manual is also a map, a hint system, a lore book, and occasionally, the solution to puzzles hidden in plain sight. The way it layers knowledge is unlike anything else in the medium.

Combat & World Design

The combat feels like a tighter, more deliberate take on the Zelda/Souls formula — simple on the surface but with surprising depth. The isometric world is dense with secrets, many hidden behind perspective tricks or cryptic environmental clues.

Who Should Play

  • Fans of Zelda, Dark Souls, and Fez
  • Anyone who misses physical game manuals
  • Players who love discovering secrets without waypoints or quest markers

The less you know going in, the better.
